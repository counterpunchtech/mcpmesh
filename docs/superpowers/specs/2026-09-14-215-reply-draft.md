## What mcpmesh-node opens to one remote

Every `Endpoint::connect` in the tree, for a paired peer in steady state:

| Site | ALPN | Trigger | Lifetime |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dial.rs` → `net::connect` | `mcpmesh/mcp/1` | **one connection per `open_session`** | dies when the session ends |
| `reach.rs:484` | `mcpmesh/ping/1` | one per probe; a probe runs only when `status`/`subscribe`/`peer_services` reads a row older than 20 s | ≈ 0.1–3.6 s, then dropped and closed |
| `Node::connect_protocol` | yours (`app/bolo-media/1`) | your call | your lifetime |
| `blobs/provider.rs` | blob ALPN | per blob fetch, hedged, first source never delayed | dies with the transfer |
| `pairing/rendezvous.rs` | `mcpmesh/pair/1` | one per ceremony | closed with `done` |

All of these are client-side on the node that dials; iroh's `open_path_on_conn` returns at once
for a server-side connection, so what your peer accepts from you never counts toward *its* K.

So for **one peer, one live mcpmesh session**: K = 1 sustained, K = 2 for ≤ 3.6 s up to three
times a minute while something polls `status` — and K = 1 flat if nothing polls. The probe
connection is out of the fan-out set the moment it drops: noq closes it implicitly, the
`OnClosed` future fires, `handle_connection_close` removes the `ConnId`, and
`open_path_on_all_conns` only iterates live entries. But a transient connection *does* multiply
while it is alive if its own `open_path` fails — which your trace shows (same address from two
`ConnId`s inside 300 µs) — and the K = 1 steady state holds the level rather than shrinking it, so
short K = 2 windows ratchet.

For **your** shape: two concurrent `bolo-mcp --peer` sessions = K = 2 sustained, because we open
one QUIC connection per session. Add a media call, K = 3 on the caller's side. Add a probe, K = 4.
That matches what you saw, and it is the answer to question 2:

**No, holding more than one client connection per remote was never necessary for MCP sessions.**
The accept side has multiplexed sessions as separate bi-streams on one connection since 0.1.0
(`run_mesh_connection` loops `accept_bi`; "a connection may carry several"). The dial side opens
one connection per session out of nothing but convenience. That is our inefficiency, not a
workaround for iroh, and fixing it needs no wire change and no upgrade on the far end.

The one connection we cannot fold in is yours: an `app/bolo-media/1` connection negotiates its
own ALPN at handshake and can never share the mesh connection. So during a call you are at K ≥ 2
by construction, and the doubling loop is live for you until iroh ships a fix. Worth knowing when
you read the numbers below.

## What we will change in mcpmesh-node

**One client connection per remote for MCP sessions**, dial side only. A weak-handle cache keyed
by peer + `mcpmesh/mcp/1`; a second `open_session` to the same peer opens a bi-stream on the
existing connection instead of a new connection. Weak, so the connection still dies with its
last session — no warm idle connections, no new keepalive semantics. Sessions that ask for a
per-session `idle_timeout_secs` keep their own connection, as that knob requires.

Then the probe: while a live session connection exists, `probe_peer` reads liveness and path off
that connection instead of dialling `mcpmesh/ping/1`. With both, a node holding a session to a
peer sits at exactly K = 1 from the mcpmesh side.

Target 0.54.0. The design is in the repo under `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-14-215-…`.

## What that does not fix — stated so nobody reads it as a fix

- The ~26 path ids/min and the recurring `RemoteCidsExhausted` on the long-lived connection are
  iroh's path lifecycle and continue at K = 1. Reuse turns a doubling deque into a steady queue on
  a slowly degrading connection; it does not stop the degradation.
- Reuse concentrates that burn on **one** connection for as long as any session is up. Without
  #4522's skip-on-`MaxPathIdReached`, that connection can reach the cap and stop opening new paths
  — no relay→direct upgrade for the rest of its life. Today a fresh session gets a fresh path-id
  space. That is the trade, and it is why #4522 is still the fix and not a nicety.
- Your media connection stays a second client connection for the length of every call.
- #213 (accept-side `is_selected()` clearing on a second connection) is the same K ≥ 2 shape seen
  from the accepting end. Reuse removes it for pure-mcpmesh sessions and not for your media
  connection; #213 keeps its own fix.
